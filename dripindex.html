Master Follow-Up Cadence — When a Warm Lead Goes Quiet

Use this for any prospect who showed interest (positive, curious, pricing, send-info) but stopped responding. 50–70% of all responses come from follow-ups — most reps quit after 1–2 attempts.

1
Nudge 1 — 2–3 business days
Same channel they replied on. Reference their interest + add new value. "Hey %%first_name%% — just wanted to follow up on our chat. [Add one new piece of value: a relevant case study, stat, or observation about their business.]"
2
Nudge 2 — 3–4 days later
Switch channels (LinkedIn → email or vice versa). Share a specific case study relevant to their ICP. "Thought this might be useful — [client name] was in a similar spot at %%company%%."
3
Nudge 3 — 5–7 days later
LinkedIn voice note or phone call. Brief, personal check-in — voice cuts through noise better than text. Keep it under 30 seconds.
4
Breakup — 2 weeks later
Final message with open door: "Hey %%first_name%% — I don't want to be a pest. If the timing's shifted, totally understand. If design ever becomes a priority at %%company%%, here's where to find us: designbees.com.au/pricing-cards. Wishing you all the best."
∞
Nurture — Monthly/Quarterly
Like and comment on their LinkedIn posts. Share value-based content (not sales). Stay visible without being pushy. This is where long-term deals come from.
⚠️
Never say: "Just checking in" / "Did you get my message?" / "I haven't heard back." Always add value — a relevant insight, case study, or observation about their business. Enterprise prospects ghost quickly and punish persistence; SMBs tolerate more follow-ups.
